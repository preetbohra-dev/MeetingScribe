For Microsoft Teams on Mac and Windows

Join the call. Hang up. The notes are written.

MeetingScribe sits quietly in the background. When Teams picks up your microphone it starts recording, and a few minutes after you leave it saves a note with a summary, action items and a transcript that knows who said what. Everything runs on your own computer.

What you get

One Markdown file per meeting, in a folder you already have.

Notes land in ~/Documents/Meeting Notes. Open them in any editor, search them with Spotlight, paste them into an email. This is what one looks like.

Named after the meetingThe title comes from the Teams window, so you can find it again.

Small talk is counted, not keptWeekend plans and the weather never make it into the note. You only see how many lines were dropped.

Voices it doesn't know yetNew people get a label. Name them once and they're recognised from then on.

Nothing is quietly deletedLines that might be an echo or a mis-hearing are tagged, not removed.

How it works

There's no record button.

Your computer knows which apps are using the microphone. MeetingScribe checks once a second, and when it's Teams, the call is on.

During the call

  1. Notices the callTeams takes the mic, and recording starts within a second or two.
  2. Records two tracksYour microphone is “Me”. Teams’ own audio is everyone else, captured from Teams alone, so your music and notifications stay out of it.
  3. Notices you leftTen seconds after Teams lets go of the mic, recording stops. Being on mute doesn’t end it.

After you hang up

  1. TranscribesWhisper large-v3-turbo on each track, with long silences cut first so it doesn’t invent text.
  2. Works out who spokeEvery second of remote audio gets a voice fingerprint. Similar ones are grouped and matched against people it has heard before.
  3. Keeps the workQwen 2.5 reads the transcript and drops the personal chat, then writes the summary and action items.
  4. Saves the noteYou get a notification. Raw audio is deleted once the note is safely written.

Speakers

It learns your colleagues’ voices.

Everyone on the other end arrives mixed into one stream, so they’re told apart by how they sound. Not sure who “Speaker 2” was? Play a ten-second clip of them, then give them a name.

The note is rewritten with the right name, and from the next meeting on they’re labelled automatically. Every confident match sharpens their voiceprint a little.

$ ./ctl.sh listen
Speaker 2 · first heard at 00:03:12
"Can we push the cutover to Monday?"

$ ./ctl.sh name "Speaker 2" "Priya Shah"
Rewrote 2026-10-28 1400 - Migration sync.md
Saved Priya Shah's voiceprint

Jira, if you use it

Action items become tickets. You approve each one.

Run ctl jira after a meeting. It drafts each piece of work, searches every Jira space you can see for tickets that already cover it, and suggests what to do.

Nothing is sent until you press y. You can edit any field first, and it remembers what it pushed, so running it twice doesn’t create duplicates.

  • CommentThe work is already in an open ticket. Add the meeting’s update to it.
  • SkipThe ticket is done and nobody said the problem is back.
  • CreateNothing covers it yet. Draft a ticket and link the related ones.

Privacy

Your meetings stay on your computer.

The models are downloaded once. After that MeetingScribe runs with the network switched off for them, and Jira is the only thing that ever talks to the internet, and only when you start it.

Audio

Written to a local app folder while the call runs, and deleted as soon as the note is saved.

Models

Whisper and Qwen run on your own hardware: MLX on a Mac, faster-whisper and llama.cpp on Windows. Ollama works too, if it’s on your machine.

Voiceprints

A few hundred numbers per person in a local file. They can’t be turned back into audio, and you can delete any of them.

Check for yourself

Watch nettop on a Mac, or Resource Monitor on Windows, while a meeting is being processed. There are no connections to see.

Install

One command. About fifteen minutes, most of it downloading.

  • Apple silicon Mac on macOS 14.4+, or Windows 11
  • 6–10 GB free
  • No admin rights
  • Python is set up for you

Mac

$ git clone https://github.com/preetbohra-dev/MeetingScribe.git
$ cd MeetingScribe
$ ./install.sh

Windows

> git clone https://github.com/preetbohra-dev/MeetingScribe.git
> cd MeetingScribe
> .\install

Type I agree when it shows the terms. A Mac asks once for the microphone and system audio; Windows asks for nothing. Then join a Teams call. The README covers permissions, settings and what to do if something doesn’t work.

Recording a meeting usually needs everyone’s agreement, and voiceprints count as biometric data in many places. Tell people you’re using it and check your company’s policy. MeetingScribe is provided as is, under the GPL-3.0 licence, and isn’t an official tool of any company.
